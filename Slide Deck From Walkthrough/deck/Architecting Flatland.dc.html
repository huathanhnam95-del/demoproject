Out of the honeymoon

Flat does not mean unstructured. It demands more discipline, not less — and everything that follows is the discipline we are importing.

The Phase 2 threshold

Month 1: alongside

Thirty days without a department chair, lesson observations or top-down approval gates. We worked in parallel, mapped each other’s strengths and found out who we turn to for what.

Phase 2: collaboration

Parallel work becomes joint work: cross-disciplinary teams at one table, decisions made in the room rather than passed between us, and a shared record everyone writes to. Flat does not mean unstructured; it demands more discipline, not less.

Month 1, in three questions

The permission reflex

Did you catch yourself waiting for permission, or waiting for someone else to tell you what to work on?

Did you worry whether you’re doing enough, because there is no boss to judge or evaluate your work?

The Valve blueprint

The promise

Desks on wheels: contributors roll to the highest-leverage bottleneck rather than wait for assignments. T-shaped teachers pair deep craft — articulatory mechanics, lesson design, audio prompting — with broad literacy across learner feedback.

The fine print

Structure happens (p. 16): informal leadership emerges instantly and hardens if unwatched. Overtime signals failed planning (p. 17). Stack ranking bred risk aversion and popularity contests (pp. 26–33) — we take the Cabals and leave the ranking.

The tyranny of structurelessness

The warning

There is no such thing as a structureless group. Abolishing formal rules does not abolish power; it hands power to whoever is charismatic and socially connected, with no vote and no appeal for anyone outside the clique.

The seven principles

Explicit delegation of specific tasks. Direct responsibility to the whole group. Distribution of authority. Rotation of demanding roles. Rational allocation by demonstrated competence. Diffusion of information. Equal access to resources.

Bureaucracy constrains from above: visible rules, visible authority, a supervisor to appeal to or dismiss.

Remove the foremen and peers build a tighter cage: mutual surveillance, chronic guilt, self-policed overwork.

The 24-hour playtest rule

Fifteen minutes of argument, then the talking stops. Both proponents build a five-minute micro-lesson, three learners run them in randomized order, and the learners’ vocal tracts settle it.

Silence behind the learner

Three learners, both prototypes in randomized order, and not one word of help from the room.

Decisions without bureaucracy

Anyone can make any curricular or technical decision, on one condition: consult the people who know the craft and the people who have to live with it. Advice is not consensus — the decider owns the outcome.

03 · Phase 2 execution

50

Interactive pronunciation modules, engineered and validated in twelve weeks. Progress is measured in validated learner performance — never in pages written or hours logged.

The twelve-week roadmap

The gates are not reviews of our documents. A sprint passes only when real learners show measurable acoustic improvement without a teacher in the room.

Forming to storming

Team effectiveness

Storming is not dysfunction — it is the friction that negotiates real standards. The fatal state is artificial harmony: agreement in the room, resentment outside it.

Situational leadership

What a lead does

Clears roadblocks within hours, facilitates the Advice Process so no step is skipped, sources learners, books observation rooms and curates the error telemetry. The lead is a shield, not a crown.

Who leads when

Leadership follows the sprint’s problem domain. The phonetician leads while we set IPA standards; the audio specialist leads while we calibrate synthesis voices. You step forward for your craft and step back for someone else’s.

Monarch to engineer

The classroom monarch

Behind a closed door, warmth and charisma carry the lesson. A furrowed brow gets an improvised metaphor within seconds, and the teacher is always in the room to rescue an ambiguous explanation.

The curriculum engineer

Nobody is in the room at 2 AM in Hanoi or Tokyo. Scaffolding has to be unambiguous, peer scrutiny is constant, and worth is measured by how reliably the materials produce mastery at scale.

The acoustic director’s desk

Audio is synthesized, then judged by ear before it enters the syllabus. The scarce resource is not studio time — it is a teacher willing to fail a file.

Three filters against synthetic slop

The blind ear test

A colleague listens with eyes closed and no text, and must identify the target word ten times out of ten.

Speech-to-text

Play the file into phone dictation. If recognition hears “sheep” for “ship”, the audio is not crisp enough.

Learner mimicry

A real learner hears the contrast and repeats it back. If not, re-prompt and generate again.

Synthesis removes vocal insecurity from critique — nobody’s voice is on trial — but default models average away tense–lax contrasts and unreleased final stops. Teachers act as acoustic directors.

04

The social contract

The objective critique protocol

Observation

“In playtest two, the learner replayed the prompt three times and hesitated seven seconds before answering.”

Diagnosis

“The /p/–/b/ contrast failed because the audio gave no crisp puff of air; “pat” sounded like “bat”.”

Proposal

“Re-prompt with an aspirated phonetic tag and add the tissue-paper drill so the puff is visible.”

Nothing is cool and nothing sucks. Subjective verdicts carry no engineering information and trigger defensiveness; observation, diagnosis and proposal carry all three.

A sustainable cadence

What you can expect

Autonomy inside your Cabal through the Advice Process. Transparent access to every file, benchmark and learner metric. Objective feedback within a day. Six focused hours a day, four days a week, and a fifth day for solo work and rest.

What you cannot expect

A daily checklist or a manager counting your hours. Consensus committees that must agree before anyone acts. Permission to bypass the Advice Process or ignore playtest data. Tolerance for back-channel politics or midnight heroism.

The fifth day

Friday stays free for solo drafting and recovery. Nobody is measured on hours logged.

The Phase 2 charter

  • We replace the illusion of structurelessness with explicit, documented structure.
  • We settle pedagogical conflict with learner prototypes, not rhetoric.
  • We lead by clearing obstacles and protecting each other’s time.
  • Cabal assignments go live this afternoon; week one drafts the living Bible.

Let’s get to work.

Fifty modules, twelve weeks, one living Bible — and a learner in the room every time we claim something works.
