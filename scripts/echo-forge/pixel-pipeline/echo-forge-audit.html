Feature audit · Echo Forge

It's a measurement harness wearing a health bar

The redesign repainted the surface. Underneath, Echo Forge is still the pronunciation lab it was built as — and every one of the three things that make a combat loop feel like a game is either missing or inverted.

Read: 11 modules, 9 sprites, 100 challenges Findings: 18 Critical: 7

The loop has three legs. All three are broken.

A combat turn works when the player makes a real choice, acts on it quickly, and gets an immediate physical answer that changes the next choice. Echo Forge breaks each leg for a different reason, which is why no amount of restyling fixes it.

Leg 01 Decide Read the situation, weigh options with real trade-offs, commit. The Warden is a number and nothing else. It has no moves to read, so Block vs Parry is a coin flip and the three attack cards differ only by a damage constant.
Leg 02 Act Execute fast enough that intent and action feel like one motion. A round runs ~15.6 seconds, of which about 2.4 are you. The rest is countdowns, silence detection, and a naked network wait.
Leg 03 React The world answers physically — impact, sound, motion, numbers. Nothing moves and nothing sounds. The HP bar teleports, the Warden never animates, there is no SFX layer at all, and the reward is a six-clause paragraph.

Findings

Grouped by the leg each one breaks. File references are to public/js/echo-forge/ unless stated.

Leg 01 Decide — the choice is decorative

Five of the six decisions the UI offers resolve to a dominant option the player can't see.

F—01
The Warden has no body and no brain
Critical

Enemy state is { hp, maxHp } — that is the entire character (core/combat-reducer.js:64). Damage is a module-level constant, ENEMY_BASE_DAMAGE = 20 (sandbox-entry.js:14). There is one enemy, it has one attack, and it always hits for 20.

The event enemy.intent.presented already fires — with an empty payload (combat-reducer.js:127). The scaffolding for intent exists and was never filled in. Because there is nothing to read, "Block or Parry" isn't a decision; it's a menu of two unrelated minigames.

Fix Give the intent event a real payload — move name, damage, and type — and render it as a telegraph above the Warden. Add 3–4 move types where Block and Parry are correct answers to different ones. This single change converts the defend phase from a coin flip into a read.
F—02
The Focus economy strangles the card system
Critical

Focus starts at 1 and caps at 3 (core/policy.js:8). The only source in the entire game is a correct Block, which returns exactly 1 (core/combat-math.js:35). Two of the three attack cards cost 1 Focus.

Over a six-round fight you can afford roughly two non-basic attacks. Every other turn you press Precision Strike because it is the only free option — so the card row that dominates the screen is, in practice, one button.

Fix Either grant +1 Focus at the start of each round, or make Focus the resource that scoring well generates. The cards should be a rotation, not a locked cabinet.
F—03
The three cards are one card with three prices
High

Precision 20 / Stress 24 / Echo 26 base damage (core/policy.js:14–39). No status effects, no enemy weaknesses, no targeting differences — the only variable is the number and which engine scores it. When Focus is available, Echo Chain strictly dominates Stress Breaker.

Fix Attach an effect to each: Precision builds Combo faster, Stress Breaker staggers the Warden's next attack, Echo Chain hits twice on a high score. Then give enemies resistances so the right card changes per fight.
F—04
Damage is perfectly deterministic
High

calculateAttackDamage() (core/combat-math.js:17) has no variance and no crit tier. The same score always produces the same number. The curve itself is good — score 50 yields 13 damage, score 90 yields 26 — but a fight with zero surprise has no texture.

Worse, that 2× spread is invisible. Nothing tells the player that pushing 75 → 90 nearly doubles their output, so the skill the game is built to teach is never surfaced as a reason to try harder.

Fix Add a crit tier above ~92 with its own sound and colour, and show the multiplier on the damage number itself — 26 ×1.3 — so the curve becomes legible through play.
Leg 02 Act — the action is slow and disembodied

Roughly 85% of a round is the player waiting for a timer or a server.

F—05
Five seconds per round are pure ceremony
Critical

Every attack opens with a hard 2-second countdown (startAutoRecordCountdown(2), sandbox-entry.js:336) and closes with a 1-second silence tail before the recording even stops (sandbox-entry.js:306). The Parry path adds another second. None of it is load time — it is deliberately inserted delay.

Fix Delete the countdown; open the mic the instant the card is chosen and show a "GO" flash. Drop the silence threshold to ~450 ms. That recovers ~3.5 s per round with no architectural change.
F—06
The network wait is naked instead of hidden
High

stopAndAnalyze() posts "Analyzing your pronunciation…" (sandbox-entry.js:445) and the presenter loops a four-frame spinner (visual/presenter.js:41). The attack animation then plays after the response lands.

This is backwards. In a game the wind-up animation is the loading state — the hero lunges while the request is in flight, and the impact frame lands whenever the score arrives.

Fix Start the wind-up on recording stop, not on response. Gate only the impact frame on the result. A 1–2 s round-trip disappears entirely inside a 1.2 s swing.
F—07
The microphone is re-acquired every attempt
Medium

makeCapture() then start() runs on every single recording (sandbox-entry.js:384–386) rather than holding one stream open for the battle, adding getUserMedia latency to each turn.

Fix Acquire once in startRun(), keep the stream, and gate recording on a flag.
Leg 03 React — nothing hits back

This group is the largest source of "it doesn't feel like a game," and it is also the cheapest to fix.

F—08
There is no juice of any kind
Critical

renderMeters() (sandbox-entry.js:158) assigns progress.value directly, so the HP bar teleports. There are no floating damage numbers, no hit flash, no screen shake, no knockback, no combo pop, no drain tween. The single most important moment in the game — landing a hit — renders as a bar quietly changing width.

Fix The highest impact-per-line work in this audit: a damage number that arcs off the target and fades, a 90 ms white flash on the struck sprite, a 6 px shake on the stage, and an eased HP tween over ~400 ms with a lagging "ghost" bar behind it. Roughly 150 lines across the presenter and the stylesheet.
F—09
The game is completely silent
Critical

There is no SFX layer anywhere in the feature. The only audio paths that exist are TTS prompt playback and microphone capture. No impact sound, no crit sting, no enemy telegraph cue, no victory or defeat music, no UI clicks.

Sound is roughly half of perceived game feel, and its absence is very likely a large part of what you are reacting to without being able to name it.

Fix Eight short WebAudio samples — light hit, heavy hit, crit, enemy wind-up, enemy impact, block success, victory, defeat — plus a low ambient loop. Under 200 KB total and a single small module.
F—10
The characters are pictograms that never move
Critical

The whole visual set is nine PNGs: one hero idle, one enemy idle, four spinner frames, and three "combat result" frames — and all three result frames are the hero. The Warden has no wind-up, no attack, no hit reaction, and no death. It is the same static 256 px icon from the first turn to the last.

Both sprites are flat vector icons rather than characters: a stick figure in a cape and an abstract shield. Nothing about them expresses state, threat, or damage.

Fix Minimum viable set per side: idle, wind-up, attack, hit, defeated. Ten frames instead of two. Even reusing the current art with squash, tilt and offset transforms would be a large improvement before any new art is commissioned.
F—11
The reward for a good hit is a lab report
High

formatAnalysisFeedback() (core/learner-feedback.js:55) joins six clauses into one paragraph, rendered on every single turn: "Score: 78/100. Azure word accuracy: 78. Pronunciation focus: initial /h/ and final diphthong. Your attempt met the action threshold. 18 damage dealt."

That is genuinely useful coaching sitting in exactly the wrong place. It occupies the instant that should belong to impact, and by round four the player has stopped reading it.

Fix Split the channels. In the moment: a verdict word and a number — PERFECT · 26. Move the phoneme evidence to the post-fight summary and an on-demand "why?" toggle, where it will actually be read.
F—12
The ultimate ability is a checkbox
Medium

Resonance Burst charges across the entire fight and then fires as #burst-toggle, a checkbox beside the attack cards (sandbox-entry.js:188). No animation, no fanfare, no confirmation that something big happened.

Fix Make it a charged button that visibly fills as Resonance climbs, pulses when ready, and triggers a distinct full-stage effect and sound on use.
Meta The run has no stakes and no memory

Even with all three legs fixed, there is currently no reason to play a second time.

F—13
The RNG seed is hardcoded — every run is identical
Critical

rng = createSeededRng(0x4543484f) — a fixed literal (sandbox-entry.js:768). Every run at a given level draws the same challenges in the same order, forever. Determinism that belongs in the test harness has shipped into the product, and it removes replay value completely.

Fix One line. Seed from Date.now() in normal play and let __ECHO_FORGE_TEST_HOOKS__ inject the fixed seed for tests — the hook system is already in place.
F—14
Nothing carries between runs
High

Victory leads to a statistics card and a "Play Again" button that resets to an identical fight. Nothing is unlocked, nothing persists, nothing was at risk. A single fight is about six rounds — too short to be a session and too flat to be a highlight.

Fix Wrap fights in a run: three escalating Wardens, HP carried between them, and a choice of reward after each — heal, add a card, or upgrade one. That is the smallest structure that turns six rounds into a session worth finishing.
F—15
The information architecture is still a laboratory
High

The entry point is sandbox-entry.js, the root is #echo-forge-root[data-state], and the dev panel offers Replay, Export JSON and Export CSV of timing telemetry. Collapsing that behind a <details> hid it; it did not change what the page is organised around, which is proving the engine works.

Fix Move telemetry behind a ?dev=1 query parameter, rename the entry module, and let the battle own the full viewport.
F—16
No onboarding for the three resources
Medium

Focus, Resonance and Combo appear as bare numbers in the meter row and are never explained. A new player's only hint is a tooltip reading "Azure word evidence · base damage 20 · No Focus cost" — engine vocabulary, not player vocabulary.

Fix A three-turn scripted opening that introduces attacking, spending Focus, and defending — then rewrite the card tooltips in player language.
F—17
There is no fiction at all
Medium

Nothing establishes what the Echo Warden is, why it is being fought, or what winning means. The name promises a world; the screen delivers two icons and a progress bar.

Fix Two or three sentences on the setup screen and one line of Warden dialogue per fight. Cheap, and it makes the sprites read as characters instead of markers.
F—18
The content pool is thin for repeat play
Medium

100 challenges total, exactly 20 per CEFR level, split across word, phrase and listening types. A single fight consumes roughly 14 of them, so a level's pool is close to exhausted in two runs — a problem that only becomes visible once F—13 is fixed and runs stop being identical.

Fix Target ~60 per level before promoting this out of sandbox. The generation pipeline in scripts/echo-forge/ already exists.

What to do, in order

Sequenced by felt improvement per hour of work. Tier 1 alone will change your answer to "does this feel like a game."

Tier 1 Make it feel physical ~1 evening · no architecture change
  1. Juice pass. Floating damage numbers, hit flash, screen shake, eased HP drain with a ghost bar. Fixes F—08.
  2. Sound pass. Eight SFX plus an ambient loop. Fixes F—09.
  3. Delete the ceremony. Drop both auto-record countdowns, shorten the silence tail to 450 ms, hold one mic stream per battle. Fixes F—05 and F—07.
  4. Move the wind-up in front of the network call so latency plays as animation. Fixes F—06.
  5. Unhardcode the RNG seed. One line. Fixes F—13.
Tier 2 Make the decisions real ~1 weekend · touches the reducer
  1. Enemy intent system. Fill the existing enemy.intent.presented payload, render a telegraph, add 3–4 Warden moves that Block and Parry answer differently. Fixes F—01.
  2. Loosen Focus and differentiate the cards with per-card effects and enemy resistances. Fixes F—02 and F—03.
  3. Crit tier and visible multipliers so the scoring curve becomes learnable through play. Fixes F—04.
  4. Sprite states. Idle, wind-up, attack, hit, defeated per side — transforms on the existing art first, new art later. Fixes F—10.
  5. Charged Resonance Burst button with its own effect. Fixes F—12.
Tier 3 Give it a reason to exist twice Ongoing · product-level
  1. Run structure. Three escalating fights, carried HP, a reward choice between each. Fixes F—14.
  2. Split the feedback channels — verdict in the moment, coaching on the summary. Fixes F—11.
  3. Meta-progression: unlocked cards, best streak, persistent per-phoneme mastery.
  4. Onboarding, fiction, and de-sandboxing the shell. Fixes F—15, F—16, F—17.
  5. Grow the catalog toward 60 challenges per level. Fixes F—18.

The one-line version

Echo Forge measures pronunciation accurately and then reports the result. A game would react to it.

Everything in Tier 1 is about replacing a report with a reaction, and none of it requires touching the combat model or the analysis engines. That is where I would start.
