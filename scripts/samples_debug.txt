BEGINNER: Keith Haring was an artist. He started by drawing graffiti in subway stations in New York. He used chalk and talked to people about his drawings. He drew a lot and became famous. People wanted to buy his art, but Keith wanted everyone to be able to see his art. So, he opened a store called the Pop Shop. Some artists didn't like that he sold his art, but the Pop Shop changed how people saw art and business.
INTERMED: Keith Haring started his career as a street artist, creating graffiti in New York subways. He would travel between stations, drawing with chalk and discussing his art with people. Haring's subway drawings helped him develop his signature style, and he became so productive that fame quickly followed. Soon, art galleries wanted to buy his work, which became very expensive. However, this went against Haring's belief that art should be accessible to everyone. To make his art more available, he opened the Pop Shop. While some street artists criticized him for 'selling out,' the Pop Shop ultimately changed perceptions of the connection between art and commerce.

INTERMED: It's challenging to compare the intelligence of different animal species; for example, how can we directly compare a dolphin and a horse? Psychologists use a method to assess intelligence that doesn't require the animal's active participation. A good indicator of intelligence is the relative size of an animal's brain. However, comparing brain sizes across species isn't straightforward, as larger animals like elephants naturally have larger brains than humans. Therefore, scientists use the Cephalization Index, which compares brain size to body size. According to this index, humans are the most intelligent, followed by great apes, porpoises, and elephants. Generally, predatory animals, such as canines, tend to be smarter than herbivores, and animals living in social groups exhibit higher intelligence and Cephalization quotients than solitary animals.

BEGINNER: People move to new places for many reasons. They might leave because of problems where they live. They might go to a new place because it is better. Some people want nicer weather. Others want cheaper homes. Some look for jobs, or they stop working. Some leave busy cities. They want a nicer place to live. They also want to be near family and friends. Often, many things make people move.
INTERMED: People relocate to new regions for a variety of reasons. The decision to move often stems from a mix of what are known as 'push and pull factors.' Push factors are those that encourage people to leave their current region, while pull factors are those that attract them to a new one. Common motivations include seeking a more desirable climate, securing more affordable housing, pursuing employment opportunities or entering retirement, escaping the congestion of city life, desiring a more agreeable environment, and wanting to be closer to family and friends. Ultimately, numerous complex and individual reasons can combine to influence a person or family's decision to relocate.
