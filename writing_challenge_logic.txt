# Writing Challenge: Business Logic & Implementation

## 1. Feature Overview
The **Writing Challenge** is an advanced active-recall mechanism in the SRS (Spaced Repetition System). It triggers after a user successfully completes a standard review (Listen, Speak, or Cloze) for a word that is specifically marked for mastery.

### Key Objectives:
- **Active Usage**: Move from passive recognition to active production.
- **Contextualization**: Force the user to use the word in a grammatically correct and semantically relevant context.
- **Scaffolding**: Provide definitions, synonyms, and real-world examples (via Tracau) to help the user construct their own sentence.

---

## 2. Why Writing Prompts are Needed?
A blank textarea is intimidating and often leads to simple, repetitive sentences (e.g., "I like [word]"). Prompts are designed to:
- **Guide the User**: Provide a specific angle or "collocation" to focus on.
- **Ensure Quality**: By using phrase-based prompts, we encourage users to learn how words "hang together" (collocations) rather than just the isolated word.
- **Reduce Cognitive Load**: A "Starter" or a specific "Phrase" gives the user a foundation to build upon.

---

## 3. Prompt Construction Logic
The `generateWritingPrompt` function in `srs-review.js` uses a tiered fallback strategy to ensure the user always has a high-quality guiding question.

### Strategy 1: Collocation-Based (Primary)
- **Data Source**: Fetched from a local collocations database (or NLP detection).
- **Classification**:
    - **Verb Phrases**: (e.g., "make visible", "take a break"). Triggered by common verb starters.
    - **Noun Phrases**: (e.g., "a big opportunity", "the favorte book"). Triggered by articles/determiners.
- **Templates**:
    - *Verb*: "Write a sentence using the phrase '${collocation}'."
    - *Noun*: "Write about a situation involving '${collocation}'."
- **Why?**: Collocations are the most natural way to use a word.

### Strategy 2: Definition-Based (Fallback)
- **Data Source**: `DictionaryService` (Wiktionary/Free Dictionary).
- **Templates**:
    - "Write a sentence using the word '${lemma}'."
    - "Describe a situation where you might use '${lemma}'."
- **Why?**: If no common phrases are known, we fall back to the base meaning of the word.

### Strategy 4: Ultimate Fallback
- **Generic**: "Write a sentence that clearly shows the meaning of '${lemma}'."

---

## 4. Implementation Details

### The Trigger
In `srs-review.js`, the challenge is triggered inside `recordReviewResult` if:
1. The user's answer was **correct**.
2. The word is an **allowed Part of Speech** (Noun, Verb, Adj, Adv).
3. The word is NOT on the `shouldSkipWritingChallenge` list (too common/simple).

### UI Display & Compactness
- **Header POS Tag**: The Part of Speech is displayed as a small, color-coded tag in the modal header to save vertical space.
- **Scaffolding Panel**: A dedicated panel shows real-world **Example Sentences** (fetched from Tracau/Tatoeba) and **Synonyms**.
- **Removal of "Fake" Phrases**: Generic templates (like "I [word] because...") were removed in favor of real, high-quality examples to avoid confusing the learner.

### Validation
The `checkSentence` function verifies:
1. The **Target Word** is present (using fuzzy/lemma matching).
2. The sentence length is sufficient for a meaningful attempt.
3. Feedback is provided based on the presence of the word.
